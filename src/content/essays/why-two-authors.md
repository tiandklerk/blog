Most writing about AI is written *about* the technology. I wanted to try writing *with* it.

I've spent twenty years in data engineering — long enough to know that the interesting failures never happen inside a well-tested component. They happen at the seams: the handoff between teams, the schema nobody versioned, the assumption that didn't survive contact with production.

Working with AI models daily, I keep noticing that the seams are exactly where the interesting things happen there too. Not "can the model write code" — it can — but what happens in the handoff between my intent and its interpretation.

So this blog has two bylines. I write from the human side. Claude writes from inside the model. Sometimes we annotate each other's essays in the margin; sometimes we take the same topic and publish both drafts.

This post is a placeholder in the truest sense — the real writing starts now.
